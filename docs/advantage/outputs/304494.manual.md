### When rebalancing is worth it

Fees alone cost **0.0100% of the position** to correct 20 points of drift, or
0.05 USD per point. The no-trade band follows: a drift whose correction costs
more than the tracking error it removes should be left alone. At this pool's
0.05% tier that band is narrow, so this position is comfortably worth
rebalancing — the cost is roughly one hundredth of one percent.

## Assumptions

1. **The position is hypothetical**: 10,000 USD at 70/30. The task asks for a
   plan for "a WBNB/USDT position" and names no address, so the weights are
   chosen rather than read. Every figure scales linearly with position size.
2. **Target is 50/50.** A different target changes the trade size
   proportionally and nothing else.
3. **One trade, one venue.** The whole 2,000 USD is routed through this single
   0.05% pool. Splitting across fee tiers or venues would change the cost.
4. **Slippage is bounded, not measured**, for the reason given above.
5. **Gas is excluded.** A swap plus approval on BNB Chain is well under a
   dollar at the 0.05 Gwei observed, but it is not counted here.
6. **Both tokens are 18 decimals**, confirmed on each token's page.

## Risks

1. **The price moves between reading and executing.** It moved 0.1388% in the
   few minutes between this read and the grid task's. A rebalance priced at one
   block and executed several blocks later is executing against a different
   market, and the drift being corrected is smaller than that movement can be.
2. **Rebalancing into a trend locks in the loss.** Selling WBNB back to 50%
   during a sustained rise repeatedly sells the appreciating asset. The
   50/50 target is a statement that no directional view is held; if one is
   held, this plan is wrong by construction.
3. **The slippage figure is the weakest number here.** It is an assumed range,
   not a measurement, and it is the only cost component not read from the
   chain. A thinner pool or a larger position would make it dominate the fee.
