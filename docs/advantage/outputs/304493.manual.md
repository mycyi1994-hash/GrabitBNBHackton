## Answer

### Annualising

`supplyRatePerBlock` is the per-block rate scaled by 1e18. Annualising needs a
blocks-per-year figure, derived from the observed block time rather than taken
from documentation:

```
blocks/year = 31,536,000 s / 0.45 s = 70,080,000
APR = (rate / 1e18) x blocks_per_year
APY = (1 + rate / 1e18) ^ blocks_per_year - 1
```

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **vUSDT** | 4.09938204e-10 | 2.8728% | **2.9145%** |
| 2 | vUSDC | 2.90684880e-10 | 2.0371% | 2.0580% |
| 3 | vDAI | 2.58617070e-10 | 1.8124% | 1.8289% |

**USDT is the best of the three, at 2.9145% APY.** The spread from top to bottom
is 1.0856 percentage points, which is wide for three assets that are all
dollar-pegged — it is a statement about borrowing demand for each, not about
their relative risk.

This is the **net supply rate**: `supplyRatePerBlock` is already net of the
market's reserve factor, so it is what a supplier actually receives on the
principal. It excludes XVS incentive rewards, which would be additive. Any
figure quoted with rewards included is not comparable to this one.

### Gas break-even

A round trip is one supply and one redeem.

```
gas cost = gas_units x 0.05 Gwei x 691.86 USD/BNB
```

Gas units are the one figure here not read from the chain, so the break-even is
given across a range:

| Round trip | Cost in BNB | Cost in USD | Break-even deposit, held 1 year | held 1 day | held 1 hour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 200,000 gas | 0.00001000 | $0.0069 | $0.24 | $86.65 | $2,079 |
| 300,000 gas | 0.00001500 | $0.0104 | $0.36 | $129.97 | $3,119 |
| 400,000 gas | 0.00002000 | $0.0138 | $0.47 | $173.29 | $4,159 |

**Gas is not the constraint here.** At 0.05 Gwei a round trip costs about one
US cent, so a deposit held for a year breaks even below a dollar. Holding
period is what matters, not size: the same round trip needs roughly $130 to
break even over a single day, and roughly $3,100 over an hour. A yield strategy
that rotates hourly is paying for gas out of a rate this thin.

## Assumptions

1. **Block time is 0.45 s**, observed on BscScan at the time of reading. BNB
   Chain has changed its block time more than once; every annualised figure
   above scales inversely with it, so a stale value would move all three
   equally and would not change the ranking.
2. **Per-block compounding.** APY compounds every block, which is what Venus's
   accrual actually does. The APR column is shown alongside so the difference
   is visible rather than hidden.
3. **Gas units are assumed**, not measured, and are given as a range for that
   reason. Measuring them needs a simulated transaction, which was outside a
   read-only task.
4. **Rewards are excluded.** XVS incentives, if any are active on these
   markets, are additive and are not counted.
5. **Three markets, Core Pool only.** Venus also runs isolated pools that may
   list USDT at different rates. This is a ranking of the Core Pool stablecoin
   markets, not of every venue on Venus.
6. **Rates are instantaneous.** Each is the rate at the block that was read.

## Risks

1. **The rate is not a promise, it is a reading.** Venus supply rates float
   with utilisation, and utilisation moves with borrowing demand. A supplier
   entering at 2.9145% earns whatever the rate averages over the holding
   period, which can be materially lower. This is the single largest gap
   between the number above and what someone would actually receive.
2. **The ranking can invert.** A 1.09 point spread across three dollar-pegged
   assets is a demand artefact, not a structural feature, and a large borrow or
   repay in any one market moves it. The ranking is valid at the source block
   and nowhere else.
3. **Gas break-even understates short holds.** The table prices only the supply
   and redeem. Approving the token, entering the market, or claiming rewards
   each add a transaction, and at hourly rotation those additions dominate the
   yield entirely.
